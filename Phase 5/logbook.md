## Logbook

- Chased an apparent rendering fault in the replay and found a real one in the simulator. The serving link line kept a single colour for a whole run while the pLOS figure annotated at its midpoint visibly swept its range. The renderer was innocent: it coloured the line from linkInfo.los, which createScenarioChannels draws once per gNB-UE pair from the INITIAL node positions and never writes again, while the annotation recomputed pLOS from the live positions each frame. So the two disagreed because the simulator itself was holding a frozen LOS state, and the display was reporting it honestly. tr36777ChannelModel reads the same frozen entry per packet; only the distance dependence of pathloss and the z-boundary crossing were tracking the UE.
- Quantified how much it mattered before fixing it, because the answer is scenario dependent and belongs in the write up. In UMa-AV between 40 and 100 m, Table B-1 never gives a probability below about 0.91, so a frozen LOS state is close to harmless there and above 100 m the probability is exactly one. UMi-AV over the same heights sweeps roughly 0.83 down to 0.20 across a cell, and terrestrial UEs span the full range. The defect therefore cost least in the scenario the aerial work concentrates on and most in UMi and on the ground.
- Made the state dynamic through a spatially consistent random field rather than a per packet redraw, which would flicker meaninglessly, or a Markov chain, which would carry state between calls. The chain is the tempting option and is the wrong one here: pathloss is evaluated per packet in whatever order the simulator delivers packets, so anything stateful makes the result order dependent and breaks the byte identical regeneration contract the dataset rests on. A field indexed by position is a pure function and cannot. It follows TR 38.901 clause 7.6.3.1 Option 2 with the Table 7.6.3.1-2 correlation distances, one field per gNB so two UEs in the same place agree about a given cell, and shadow fading rides two further fields with sigma re-read for the live state.
- Had to correct the interpolation before it was usable. Bilinear interpolation of independent normals does not preserve the marginal: its variance runs from 0.25 at a cell centre to 1 at a grid node, so a threshold test against it would be biased by where in the cell the UE happened to be. Dividing by the norm of the interpolation weights restores exactly unit variance at every point, and mapping through the normal CDF with erfc rather than normcdf keeps the run free of a Statistics toolbox dependency. Verified numerically: pooled over forty fields the uniform variate has mean 0.500 and variance 0.0831 against a target of 0.0833, with every decile within about one per cent of a tenth of the mass. The correlation is 0.95 at a fifth of the correlation distance, 0.25 at one and zero beyond two, so the correlation distance is right and the shape is not exponential; that is written up as a known limitation rather than papered over.
- Made linkState the single entry point for LOS state, called by both the channel model and the replay renderer, so the colour on screen is by construction the state the simulator used. The two can no longer drift apart, which is the class of bug this started as. The replay also gained a losState_serving trace, so the state can be plotted directly against pLOS_serving, and the annotation now prints LOS or NLOS and tags it frozen for an archived Phase 4 bundle, which replays unchanged through the fallback path.
- Wrote phase5_ValidateLOS to pin all of it down, including a regression test that the state actually moves: a UE flown 1.6 km at 50 m in UMi must change state, and it produces about fourteen transitions per field against zero for the old code. The statistical checks are pooled across forty fields deliberately. A single realisation of a correlated field over a square kilometre holds only a few hundred independent cells, so its spatial mean and variance scatter by about 0.05, and a tight tolerance on one realisation would fail correct code roughly a third of the time. Every threshold in that file was set from the observed spread over sixty repeated trials rather than guessed. The trajectory test uses UMi rather than UMa for the same reason: UMa's probability barely leaves 0.9 in that band, so it would have been a flaky test rather than an informative one.
- Settled the question of whether an LOS flag belongs in the feature CSV. It does not, and the reasoning is worth recording because the column is tempting. An operator cannot observe LOS state at all; it is a latent property of the environment and nothing in the reporting chain carries it. It is also near collinear with the label here, since pLOS is exactly one above 100 m in UMa, so it would leak ground truth and inflate every Phase 6 score. What goes in instead are its observable consequences: across subband CQI spread for frequency flatness, lag one autocorrelation and fade rate of serving SINR for the fading regime, and rank statistics for rank collapse. The state itself is summarised by losDiagnostics into the run summary and the replay bundle, where it is available for analysis and cannot reach a classifier.
- Audited what else the operator could see and was not being given, and found most of it already in the logs and being thrown away at extraction. The rank indicator was written to the scheduler context log and dropped. Granted resource blocks likewise. The per cell SINR snapshot in handoverManager.sinrLog had been written since Phase 3 for offline plotting and never once read, though it is the most direct measurement available of the elevated UE geometry, namely how many cells a UE hears at comparable strength. Retransmissions were not captured at all because only the new transmission scheduler hooks were instrumented, so the residual BLER, a standard operator KPI, was invisible. And the Phase 3 block kept only the mean and variance of serving SINR, discarding the shape of the trajectory that a climb or descent produces.

The Phase 4 set stays an exact prefix, so the prefix chain from Phase 3 upward is intact and existing files remain valid truncations. The 21 July decision to hold the schema at the Phase 4 columns is superseded; since the LOS fix forces a regeneration anyway, doing both in one pass costs one regeneration rather than two.
- Extended cqiLoggingScheduler for the quantities that were genuinely missing rather than merely discarded: across subband CQI spread, timing advance, buffer occupancy, the retransmission flag, granted layers and HARQ process. Timing advance and buffer occupancy are probed by name from the R2024b UEContext because their layout there is not documented, so they may come back NaN; that is reported by the smoke check and the all NaN column is dropped by the loader rather than imputed to a constant, which would have left a dead predictor distorting the importance rankings. Retransmission grants are now caught two ways, through the previously uninstrumented scheduleRetransmissions hooks and through the redundancy version on the grant itself, so a release exposing only one of them still yields the BLER proxy.

It now derives the predictor list from the CSV header by removing a fixed exclusion list, and asserts that nothing matching a LOS flag or a raw position, altitude or true speed quantity is present, since a derived list would otherwise absorb a leaking column just as happily as a useful one.

The CQI, MCS, rank, resource, reliability and timing advance blocks are all weaker than their real network equivalents for that reason.

Dispatch is now one run per worker at a time, through parforOptions with RangePartitionMethod fixed and SubrangeSize 1, so a worker takes the next queued run at the moment it finishes one. On this batch's estimated costs it takes the finish from 92 h to 76 h against seed order, because seed order leaves 46 h runs starting in the second wave behind 30 h ones; the ideal from total CPU time over twelve workers is 70 h. Skipped runs are dispatched first since they cost nothing and clear their bars at once. Because the loop variable is now the queue position rather than the run index, each result record carries its own run index and the client puts the results back into seed order before the manifest is built; parfor will not accept an output indexed by anything but the loop variable.
